One page for the whole house.

I had an app for the lights, another for the Echos, one for the heating, one for the TV and one each for the consoles. So I built a single page that shows the lot. It runs on an old Windows PC in the corner, and works on a laptop, a tablet or a phone.

What it does

It's the things I actually reach for. Everything updates on its own, and anything you can change, you change with one tap.

The front door

When someone rings, every screen in the house shows it within seconds, with a chime, and a light can glow to catch your eye. Tap Live view to see who's there, with sound, and save a picture. No Ring subscription needed.

Lights and plugs

Every light and plug that Alexa knows about, with dimming, and colours and warm-to-cool white for colour bulbs. It goes through Alexa, so there are no extra apps or keys to set up.

Echos

Which ones are online, what's playing with play, pause and skip, the volume, Do Not Disturb, and the next alarm or timer.

Routines

My Alexa routines as buttons. Goodnight, film night, leaving the house. One tap instead of talking to the kitchen.

TV and consoles

Turn the Samsung TV on and off, change the volume and open apps. It says what's on, even on the BT box: Netflix, YouTube, streaming or live TV. Chromecasts show what's being cast, and the PS5 shows which game is on.

Weather

Now, the next few hours and the next five days, with a plain line saying when the rain is due, and real thermometer readings from nearby airfields to check the forecast against. Free from Open-Meteo and aviationweather.gov, no accounts needed.

Heating and more

The room temperature from my heating dashboard, the front door camera, and a tile that opens the F1 race dashboard in its own window.

Who's home

Everyone's phone on the home Wi-Fi shows them as home, right at the top of the page. It waits ten minutes before saying someone's out, because phones nap off the Wi-Fi.

Computers

Which computers are on, which are busy and how fast, plus processor, memory, disk space and even the Raspberry Pi's temperature.

Day, Night or Colour

Pick the look that suits the room. At night, the lights that are on glow on the screen. Fold away the cards you don't use; each phone remembers its own.

How it works

It's a small Python program running on an old Windows 11 PC that was gathering dust. It talks to everything over the home network and serves the page on port 8095, so anyone at home can open it.

Alexa does most of the heavy lifting. The smart plugs and bulbs are already in the Alexa app, so the dashboard switches them through Alexa rather than needing a separate login for each brand.

Away from home, a Cloudflare tunnel puts it on my own web address, behind a PIN. Nothing is opened up on the router.

The whole thing starts itself when the PC logs on, and restarts if it ever falls over.
